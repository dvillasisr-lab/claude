# Consulting deck

Structure the story first, then the slides. Hand off rendering to the slide tool the session has (the Slides artifact type, or the `pptx` skill when a .pptx file is requested).

## 1. Storyline before slides

1. Ask (or infer) the audience, the decision they must make, and the one answer.
2. Write the governing thought in one sentence: the answer, not the topic.
3. Build the pyramid (Minto): 3 to 5 key supporting arguments, each backed by evidence. MECE: no overlaps, no gaps.
4. Open with SCQA: Situation, Complication, Question, Answer.
5. Draft the "ghost deck": only the action titles, in order. Read them top to bottom. If they tell the whole story on their own, the structure works. Only then build slides.

## 2. Slide rules

- **Action title**: a full sentence with the takeaway, at most 2 lines, ideally with a number. "Online sales grew 34% while stores fell 8%", never "Sales overview".
- **One message per slide.** Everything on the slide proves the title. If it doesn't, cut it or move it to the appendix.
- **Exhibit first**: a chart or a table beats bullets. Choose the chart by the message: change over time = line or column; composition = stacked bar or waterfall; ranking = horizontal bar; correlation = scatter.
- **Highlight the point**: one accent color on the data that proves the title, everything else gray.
- **Source line** at the bottom of every exhibit: "Source: ...; analysis". Add notes for definitions and assumptions.
- **Tracker** (optional) in the top corner showing the section.
- Bullets: at most 3 to 5, parallel grammar, no full paragraphs.
- Numbers: consistent units, rounding, and time periods. Label axes and units directly on the chart, not in a legend.

## 3. Standard flow

1. Title slide
2. Executive summary: the full storyline on one slide, as the action titles grouped by argument
3. Context / situation (short)
4. One section per key argument, each opening with a divider that restates the argument
5. Recommendation and next steps: who, what, by when
6. Appendix: backup analyses, methodology, detailed data

## 4. Visual style

- White background, dark text, one brand accent plus grays. Sans serif (the client's font if one exists).
- Generous margins, strict grid, left-aligned text, same positions for title, body, and source on every slide.
- No clip art, no stock photos, no 3D charts, no gradients, no shadows.
- Use harvey balls, 2x2 matrices, waterfalls, and Gantt bars for frameworks, prioritization, bridges, and roadmaps.

## 5. Final check

- Read only the titles: is the story complete and in order?
- Does every slide prove its title, with a cited source?
- Does the executive summary match the body?
- Are the next steps concrete, with owners and dates?
