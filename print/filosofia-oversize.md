# Oversize

**Philosophy:** The subject is too big for the page, and the page admits it. The machine does not sit inside the frame; it breaks it, crosses the fold and keeps going on the other side. Scale is the message.

**Space and form.** One monumental subject, cropped the way a magazine cover editor would crop it: tight, confident, bleeding off the edge. Behind it, a single word set at the size of architecture, partly swallowed by the subject so the eye completes it. Foreground and type interlock in depth, image over word, never beside it. The two faces of the object are one continuous panorama, so turning it over is part of the reading.

**Color and material.** Carbon black ground, one industrial yellow used at full strength and only once. On the reverse the same word returns as black on black, a gloss varnish ghost that is felt more than seen. Photography stays true and unfiltered; the material honesty of matte paint, machined aluminum and rubber carries the luxury.

**Scale and rhythm.** Extreme contrast of size: letterforms hundreds of points tall against captions set small, tracked wide and quiet. Everything secondary is pushed to the edges and aligned to one hairline, so the monumental center has room to breathe.

**Craft.** Every crop, every alignment and every tracking value must look argued over for days, the product of master-level editorial judgment. Nothing decorative is added. The work should feel inevitable, as if no other composition were possible.
