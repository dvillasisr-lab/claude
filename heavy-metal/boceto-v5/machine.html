Questions from the fence line

What's worth knowing before you watch it pull.

Why a Cat V8, when almost nobody runs one?

Most Pro Stock tractors run an inline six. Chris went the other way: a Caterpillar 3208 V8 in reverse flow, a setup you won't find on any other tractor on the track. That's the point. Nothing about it is off the shelf, and nothing about it was quick.

What is Pro Stock, exactly?

One of the diesel classes in tractor pulling. The tractors still look like farm tractors, run a single diesel engine and hook up at a set maximum weight. The exact limits on engine size, turbo and weight are set by each league's rulebook (NTPA, PPL).

How much does it weigh, and why does that matter?

10,000 LB, front weight box included. Every tractor in a class hooks up at the same maximum weight, so being heavier is not an advantage. Where the weight sits is the game: weight up front keeps the nose down and the rear tires biting as the sled gets harder to drag.

What is a full pull?

Dragging the sled all the way to the end of the track. When more than one tractor goes the distance, those tractors come back for a pull-off. Heavy Metal is still chasing its first one.

What does the sled actually do?

It's a weight transfer sled. Once the tractor starts pulling, a box of weights on the sled moves forward, from over the sled's wheels toward the pan up front. That pushes the pan into the dirt, so every foot is harder than the one before.

Who drives it?

Chris F.: owner, driver and crew chief, with more than 20 years in the sport. He built it with his family in their shop in Waterloo, WI.

Upcoming pulls are on the Schedule. Pit access depends on each event: some pulls open the pits to fans, others need a pit pass. Check with the organizer, and if you find us, come say hi.
